# Principles

Every creative choice (layout, sound design, music structure, unit lengths, pacing devices) is decided
per concept from these principles and written into that concept's storyboard for approval. Nothing from
a past video is a default. Each principle carries its reason, so it transfers to a new product.

**1. Native sound first.** Speech and ambience come from the generation itself, because separately made
voices never sit on the lips and a separately made SFX layer sounds pasted on. Anything added in post
must earn its place.

**2. Added sound only when it carries story information the picture alone can't.** Test each candidate:
does the audience need to REGISTER this event (a product moment, a message arriving, money landing)? If
yes, sound it. If it's decoration (graphics sliding in or out, wipes, trackers, stamps), leave it silent;
decoration sounds read as noise and clutter dialogue. Never put a sound on a brand word or key line. The
genre can change the answer (a comedy sting, a sound-driven concept), so list every added sound in the
storyboard for approval.

**3. Music is one continuous piece across every splice.** If the video is built from several
generations, generate them WITHOUT music and compose one score to the final cut; music baked into
separate takes jumps or restarts at every join and at the outro. The score follows the story's energy
(build, release, payoff), ducks under dialogue, never stops abruptly and carries into the outro.
Text-to-music models ignore timecodes in one long prompt, so compose one section per story movement and
stitch them at picture cuts.

**4. Choose the storytelling device from the story's structure.** Parallel storylines that affect each
other can use simultaneity (split screen, picture-in-picture, a shared screen), because hard cuts between
two worlds flip the audio and pull the viewer out. If you use one: each world is its own generation,
frame each world for its crop, keep the primary world's sound on top and the secondary world's lower,
and all dialogue at full level. A single-world story needs none of this.

**5. Generation units follow risk.** Long continuous takes (up to 30 s) give flow and consistent
performances; short units give control. Put anything that must be exact (brand names, the CTA, a claim, a
tricky physical action) in a short unit so it can be re-rolled cheaply; keep brand-free lines in the long
takes. One world per unit. Fix problems with short first-frame inserts, not by re-rolling a good long take.

**6. Brand and invented words need phonetic prompting and proof.** Spell them phonetically in the line
with a pronunciation note (`references/prompting.md`), and verify on the isolated vocal stem with two
transcribers; a full mix with music or SFX makes transcribers mishear even correct audio.

**7. Keep voices and faces consistent across generations.** Pass voice-reference clips (isolated vocal
stems) for every recurring speaker, and identity cards and keyframes for every face, into every unit they
appear in. Verify voices; say plainly when a check is inconclusive.

**8. Dialogue pacing sounds human.** Keep natural breaths between speakers (about a second, unless an
overlap is deliberate). On-camera lines are never re-timed (lip sync); off-camera lines (radio, phone,
commentary, narration) can be re-spaced. When time runs short, buy it with picture (a held or slowed
beat, a cutaway) or a deliberate overlap, never by squeezing.

**9. Every line is performed.** Write the delivery for each line as adjectives; a quotable phrase in a
note gets spoken aloud. Show a speaker on camera on their first line so the audience knows who's
talking. Cast for the market the user names.

**10. Physics and continuity are non-negotiable.** Objects keep one physical state per shot (phones don't
flip, vehicles move like vehicles). Frame-scan every take, and write the physical rules for the
concept's hardest action into the prompt.

**11. The world is fictional and dense.** Invented brands, events, rivals and sponsors on supplied
boards; every other sign blank; the only real brand is the product's own. Scan for real-brand leaks
(they happen) and crop or reject them.

**12. Motion graphics show the real product and serve the story.** Build them from the product's real
page captures, imagery and brand kit; snappy (short entrances and holds), in free space and never over a
face (re-check after any retime); numbers agree across graphics and come only from approved claims.

**13. The edit is frame-exact.** One frame rate end to end (24 fps), exact frame counts per segment,
cuts on native cuts where possible; any time warp moves picture, ambience and voice together.

**14. Claims come from the user's approved ledger only.** Every figure or product promise traces to an
approved claim with a source. No invented ratings, results, customers or testimonials.
